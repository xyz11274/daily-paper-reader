# Entropy-Gated Belief Coordination for Decentralized Multi-Agent Search Under Intermittent Communication 

Mohamed Abdelnaby, Kevin Leahy 

Department of Robotics Engineering, Worcester Polytechnic Institute 

{mabdelnaby, kleahy }@wpi.edu 

Abstract —We study decentralized multi-agent target search where homogeneous agents communicate intermittently at Poisson-distributed times. Standard unconditional belief fusion wastes communication opportunities by synchronizing agents during high-entropy exploration, when diverse independent be-liefs provide better coverage than a premature consensus. We introduce entropy-gated belief coordination , in which agents skip fusion while their collective entropy ratio exceeds a thresh-old θ and merge only during exploitation, consistent with the bifurcation structure of nonlinear opinion dynamics and the submodular structure of the per-step information gain objective. We further derive Istep (α, β ), the expected mutual information per observation step between two agents’ binary sensors, as an interpretable, communication-free measure of sensor informa-tiveness that motivates the gating design and guides system-level analysis. Experiments across 103,680 trials (nine grid sizes up to 

100 ×100 , Poisson communication timing, four target movement patterns) show that the Entropy-Gated Trust-Decay Planner (EG-TDP), which adds a detection-probability planner switch in exploitation mode, achieves mean belief quality ¯Q = 0 .300 

(mean belief mass at the true target cell, averaged across all trials and steps), a 58 .9% gain over arithmetic mean and a 37 .4% 

gain over visit-weighted fusion. On representative configurations, EG-TDP also outperforms a joint-Bayesian reference that uses all agents’ observations at every step, despite operating under random intermittent contact only. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Coordinated multi-agent search arises in search-and-rescue, environmental monitoring, and inspection, where a team must locate a target of interest with noisy sensors and intermittent communication. Each agent maintains a probabilistic belief map updated via Bayesian recursion from local observations. At communication events, agents fuse beliefs to pool indepen-dently gathered evidence. The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence provides a principled framework to decide how to combine their beliefs into a single consensus map when agents meet. The minimizer of a weighted forward KL objective is the arithmetic mean and the minimizer of the reverse KL is the geometric mean [5], [6]. Our prior work [1] applied these classical results to multi-agent tracking and introduced visit-weighted fusion to suppress ghost targets, with an implementation linear in the number of agents and the size of the environment. The present paper asks instead when agents should fuse, and how the planner should respond to the team’s information state. 

This work was supported by ONR under grant N00014-25-1-2225. 

Two problems motivate this work. First, fusing beliefs dur-ing high-entropy exploration synchronizes agents who should be maintaining diverse, complementary views of the search space. Premature consensus forces all agents to plan from the same belief, causing them to select correlated positions and undermining their search efforts. Second, a natural response to stale beliefs from long communication blackouts is temporal trust decay , in which the merged belief is weighted toward a prior or uniform distribution in proportion to elapsed blackout time on the grounds that older evidence is less reliable. However, the decay factor cannot distinguish between a correct peak and a ghost peak, because both produce the same entropy. Therefore, this method cannot be applied directly to the belief map. In this work, we 1) derive Istep (α, β ), a communication-free constant com-putable from the sensor false-positive rate α and false-negative rate β alone, that quantifies how quickly two homogenous agents’ beliefs diverge during a communi-cation blackout and informs the design of the coordina-tion policy; 2) introduce entropy-gated merging ( EG-KL ), in which agents fuse beliefs only when team entropy falls below threshold θ, preserving exploration diversity and decou-pling performance from communication frequency; 3) propose EG-TDP , which adds a planner switch from entropy-reduction to detection-probability scoring upon entering exploitation mode, enabling convergence at large scales where entropy-reduction Model Predictive Control (MPC) alone cannot commit to pursuit within the step budget, while reducing inter-agent belief trans-mission by over 97% at 100 ×100 scale; and 4) propose EG-Istep , a variant of EG-TDP that replaces the binary planner switch with a continuous blend weighted by γ ∈ (0 , 1] , a trust-decay factor derived from Istep 

that decays with blackout length to discount detection-probability scoring as beliefs grow stale, providing a sensor-dependent criterion for choosing between contin-uous and hard-threshold planner switching. II. R ELATED WORK 

Our work sits at the intersection of distributed belief fusion, multi-robot search under communication constraints, and co-arXiv:2610.07432v1 [cs.RO] 5 Oct 2026 ordination under intermittent connectivity. We describe each area and the specific gap that motivates our approach. A substantial body of work addresses how agents should combine probability distributions when they meet. Classical opinion pooling theory establishes that logarithmic pooling (geometric mean) is externally Bayesian when agents share a likelihood model, while linear pooling (arithmetic mean) is preferred under potential bias [5]–[7]. Covariance inter-section [8] provides conservative Gaussian fusion guarantees under unknown correlations, and event-triggered schemes [9], [11] reduce communication overhead while maintaining sta-bility. None of these works poses the conditional question of whether agents should adopt a shared belief at all on a given event, or how that belief should alter path planning. Our prior work [1] derived KL-optimal pooling rules and visit-weighted variants for binary-sensor tracking but applied fusion unconditionally, leaving open the question of when fusion is beneficial and how the planner should respond. Several approaches address multi-robot active information gathering under limited communication. Lauri et al. [12] formulate the problem as a Dec-POMDP but require complete knowledge of target dynamics and treat communication as absent rather than Poisson-distributed. Deep reinforcement learning approaches such as [13] learn cooperative strategies end-to-end but are sensitive to the training environment and do not address when to share beliefs. Krause et al. [3] and Corah and Michael [10] establish near-optimal greedy strategies under submodular information objectives. Earlier treatments of decentralized probabilistic search established the problem structure underlying this literature [15]–[17]. Across this literature, agents share information freely and the shared representation is immediately used for planning. The work most directly related to ours is Ristic and Skvortsov [14], who study decentralized area-restricted search with intermittent information and consensus coordination. When agents communicate they always fuse. Hollinger et al. [18] address how to fuse during multirobot search by weighting observations by communication channel quality, but the fusion decision is treated as fixed whenever agents meet. Roth et al. [19] adopt a multi-agent POMDP framework with stronger optimality guarantees, but joint policy compu-tation becomes intractable at the grid scales we consider. The question of whether to fuse is never raised, and the planner objective does not change with collective confidence. Event-triggered control [9], [11] determines when to communicate but treats the fusion rule and its planning consequence as fixed. Our work separates these decisions. Given that a Poisson event has fired, should the team merge beliefs, and how should that change the way agents plan? Deciding when to merge beliefs and how to plan with the result is a form of the exploration-exploitation tradeoff. Bizyaeva et al. [2] formalize this as a bifurcation between a disagreement regime, where agents maintain diverse indepen-dent views, and an agreement regime, where they commit to a shared estimate, controlled by a sensitivity parameter. This motivates using the team’s collective entropy as a coordination signal, formalised in Section IV-C. The opinion dynamics model operates on continuous scalar opinions with no plan-ning component. Translating the bifurcation insight into an operational policy for discrete belief maps, coupled with a planning objective that adapts when the regime switches, is the contribution of this paper. IV. T ECHNICAL APPROACH 

Effective multi-agent search requires two distinct coordina-tion modes. During exploration , agents’ beliefs are still diffuse and each agent should independently gather information from different regions of the grid. Forcing a shared belief at this stage causes agents to plan correlated paths and waste cover-age. During exploitation , beliefs have concentrated sufficiently that coordinating toward the most probable target location is more valuable than continued independent search. Our method detects this transition from a scalar summary of the team’s collective uncertainty, the entropy ratio Hratio , defined in Section IV-C, and uses a threshold θ to decide when to switch modes. B. Belief Divergence During Blackouts 

During a communication blackout each agent independently updates its local belief from its own observations. Without sharing these updates, agents searching different parts of the grid can develop very different views of where the target is, even though both are reasoning correctly from their respective evidence. The longer the blackout, the more these individual beliefs can diverge. This section derives a single sensor-level constant that quantifies how fast that divergence happens, computable from the sensor parameters alone without any inter-agent communication. Consider two agents each observing the same cell indepen-dently through sensor (1). Let p ∈ [0 , 1] be the prior proba-bility that the target occupies that cell. C. Entropy-Gated Merging (EG-KL) 

The central question is when agents should pool their beliefs. Fusing during exploration synchronizes agents who should cover complementary regions and reduces collective coverage, fusing during exploitation amplifies a shared sig-nal and coordinates pursuit. This matches the disagreement– agreement bifurcation of nonlinear opinion dynamics [2]. The per-step information gain is monotone submodular in agent observation positions [3], [4], so independent greedy planning is approximately optimal for single-step coverage. Premature consensus forces correlated position choices, reduc-ing coverage toward what a single agent achieves. The entropy gate preserves the diversity that makes independent planning effective. D. Entropy-Gated Trust-Decay Planner (EG-TDP) 

EG-TDP augments the gated merge with a planner objec-tive switch at the exploitation threshold. The rationale is as follows. During exploration ( Hratio ≥ θ), the team has not yet localized the target, the appropriate goal is to maximize information gain across the grid, and agents use standard entropy-reduction MPC independently. During exploitation (Hratio < θ ), beliefs have concentrated and the priority shifts to directing agents toward the most probable target location. E. EG-Istep: Istep -Modulated Planner 

EG-I STEP shares the same entropy gate as EG-KL and EG-TDP but replaces the binary planner switch with a continuous blend modulated by the trust-decay factor γ (10). After k steps without communication, actions are scored by:

The detection score bd depends on the pre-blackout merged belief bmerged , while d∆H uses each agent’s current local belief only. Thus γ controls how much the planner relies on bmerged . At k=0 , γ=1 and the planner pursues the merged estimate, as k grows and bmerged grows stale, γ decays and the planner shifts toward exploration to gather fresh evidence. A. Setup 

We evaluate on discrete grids ranging from 10 ×10 to 

100 ×100 (nine sizes), with N ∈ {2, 3} agents per trial. All agents use binary sensors with false positive rate α=0 .1

and false negative rate β=0 .2, giving Istep =0 .114 bits. Com-munication follows a Poisson process with mean inter-event duration ¯k ranging over {5, 10 , 25 , 50 , 100 , 200 } steps. We test four target movement patterns: stationary, random walk, evasive (moving away from the map centre), and patrol (a fixed corner-to-corner circuit). The Gaussian prior spread is 

σ0=0 .15 min( R, C ), scaling with grid size so the initial belief covers a similar fraction of the search area regardless of environment scale. At each step the planner evaluates 50 randomly sampled joint actions with horizon H=3 and selects the highest-scoring candidate. Each configuration is run for 30 trials with a step budget of 2500, yielding 12 ,960 trials per method and 103 ,680 total. The entropy threshold is θ=0 .6 and the overlap penalty weight is λov =0 .5.

B. Baselines and Comparisons 

The unconditional baselines merge at every communication event without any entropy check. A RITH uses the arithmetic mean. G EOM uses the geometric mean, which aggressively concentrates mass but can suppress recovery from incorrect peaks. VW-KL applies the visit-weighted formula (7) from [1] to reduce ghost targets. All three apply the same merge rule regardless of the team’s current uncertainty. 

EG-KL (ours) adds the entropy gate, applying (7) only when Hratio < θ, and agents plan independently with entropy-reduction MPC otherwise. EG-TDP (ours) addition-ally switches the planner to the detection-probability objec-tive (13) in exploitation, directing agents toward the high-probability region rather than continuing broad coverage. 

EG-Istep (ours) uses the same entropy gate as EG-KL and EG-TDP but replaces the binary planner switch with the continuous γ-weighted blend (14) described in Section IV-E. The F ULL -C OMM reference applies a joint Bayesian update at every step, it is included to provide a reference for the ceiling performance achievable with unconstrained communi-cation. VI. B. Scaling with Grid Size 

Figure 5 shows ¯Q across all nine grid sizes. At 10 ×10 , EG-KL ( ¯Q=0 .398 ) leads EG-TDP ( ¯Q=0 .371 ). At small scale, entropy-reduction MPC alone is sufficient to converge on the target. As grid size grows, the two methods diverge sharply. At 100 ×100 , EG-TDP maintains ¯Q=0 .218 (54% success) while EG-KL collapses to ¯Q≈0.003 (45% success), a gap of over 70 ×. VW-KL ( ¯Q=0 .003 ) and A RITH ( ¯Q=0 .001 ) also collapse at this scale. Only EG-TDP and G EOM ( ¯Q=0 .206 )maintain meaningful quality at 100 ×100 . The failure of EG-KL to scale despite entropy gating demonstrates that the planner switch, and not gating alone, enables convergence within a fixed step budget at large scale. The 70 × ¯Q gap does not translate proportionally to success rate (45% vs 54%) because at this scale success depends primarily on spatial coverage. In other words, for large grids with not enough step budget, if we found the target was the agents acting based on informative belief, or did they lose track of where the target is. This is where ¯Q differentiate what success rate alone cannot, even if methods have similar success rates.

H. Why Trust Decay in the Belief Map Fails 

Applying γ directly as bmerged =γb visit + (1 −γ)bprior re-duces ¯Q to 0.021 (TD-KL) and 0.033 (TD-KL-A), reductions of 83% and 74% below A RITH . The failure is structural, γ

is identical for a correct peaked belief and a ghost because both produce the same entropy. Moving the trust signal to the planner objective ( EG-TDP ) avoids this entirely. VII. C ONCLUSION 

We introduced entropy-gated belief coordination for decen-tralized multi-agent target search under intermittent commu-nication. The entropy ratio Hratio signals the exploration– exploitation transition. Below threshold θ the team merges be-liefs and, in EG-TDP, switches to detection-probability plan-ning. We derived Istep (α, β ) as a communication-free measure of per-step belief divergence. Across 103,680 trials, EG-TDP achieves ¯Q=0 .300 (+58 .9% over arithmetic mean), scales to 100 ×100 grids where unconditional methods collapse, and outperforms a joint-Bayesian reference on representative configurations.
